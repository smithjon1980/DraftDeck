# BOSS structural correspondence — Scaling Roadmap
Status: CANDIDATE / NOT_TESTED

## Core source
The opening stage map on PDF page 2 lists ten stages: IMPROVISE, MONETIZE, ADVERTISE, STABILIZE, PRIORITIZE, PRODUCTIZE, OPTIMIZE, CATEGORIZE, SPECIALIZE, CAPITALIZE. Each stage uses business functions, constraints and a graduation criterion. Stage 0 pp.6–9 centers on free trials and feedback with minimal technology; Stage 1 pp.14–20 centers on a first paid sale; Stage 2 pp.27–32 centers on consistent marketing and quality; Stage 3 p.58 introduces the owner-to-trainer challenge.

## Architectural correspondence boundary
The source's **commercial scaling stages** are market maturity descriptions and do not replace BOSS SEVEN Survey→Evaluate→Validate→Execute→Normalize. A shop may remain small and healthy without graduating through all stages. Headcount thresholds are descriptive/promotional, not mandatory deployment triggers. No outside framework may amend K0–K4, G1–G5 or Zone C authority.
 
## Blind spots
1. *First user evidence* is an explicit entry criterion. Day Zero/Market 7 should record observed value before revenue claims; a free trial is not proof of willingness to pay.
2. *Graduation criteria* can become independent synthetic falsification probes, not automatic stage promotion.
3. *SOHO role transitions* matter before infrastructure complexity. A one-chair operator may need only a documented handoff rather than staffing tools.
4. *Stage-conditional tech procurement*: the early chapters recommend computer, email and free tools before specialized systems. That confirms additive PocketBase/Go/HTMX/Windmill/Railway based on need, not enterprise-first architecture.
5. *Marketing and delivery* need separate state/evidence accounting. A reliable workflow does not independently establish commercial demand.
