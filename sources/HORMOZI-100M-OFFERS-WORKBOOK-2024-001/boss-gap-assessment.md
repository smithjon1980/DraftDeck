# BOSS blind-spot review — workbook cycle
Status: SPECIFIED_CANDIDATE / NOT_TESTED

Unlike the earlier book's explanatory chapters, the 2024 workbook exposes how a user would *practice* the concepts. Source pages 10–11 ask for journey times and effort; pp.21–22 and the hand-drawn continuum on p.21 distinguish service delivery formats and the sales/fulfillment tradeoff; pp.25–28 operationalize pruning and bundling; pp.29–32 cover capacity/urgency/bonuses; pp.33–34 guarantees/naming.

## New blind spots
1. **Exercise answers are missing evidence**, not operational proof; create an explicit UNANSWERED / HUMAN_SUPPLIED / OBSERVED / VERIFIED chain.
2. **Customer time includes total duration and burden**, not merely a wait-time estimate. Market 7 queue calculations require on-site and remote outcomes as separate metrics.
3. **Offer delivery vehicle is not a software stack.** A personal human intervention may be better for one-chair shops than building another agent.
4. **Real scarcity and partner value** need independent capacity/consent checks; inflated valuations and fabricated urgency must be rejected.
5. **Social correspondence:** the human sets customer priorities, constraints and refusals; the agent presents an editable comparison, not automatically generated sales claims.
6. **Capability correspondence:** people/agents/skills/tools assignments remain separate; an exercise cannot confer tool entitlements or customer approval.

No new governing K0–K4 policy, production authority or Source Admission claims.
