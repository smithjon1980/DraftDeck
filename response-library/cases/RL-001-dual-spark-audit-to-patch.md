# Case — What the Dual-Spark critique changed

## Starting challenge
The deterministic DraftDeck rendering pipeline attempted to supply its own creative composition, resulting in unsatisfactory output. The revised idea separated authentic NotebookLM source, generated creative choices, human-selected spark, downstream planning and manufacturing.

## Gemini response as relayed by human
In a user-pasted **"Diagnostic Audit: DraftDeck Dual-Spark Architecture"**, Gemini endorsed the structural division, then criticized weak human selection and an insufficient scene manifest. Specific recommendations reported included:
- Record a **human selection rationale**, not a passive click.
- The supervising planner should create a **Scene Manifest, Narrative Brief and Worker Acceptance Criteria**.
- Keep provenance with concrete byte hashes rather than filenames alone.
- Give Story, Art and Text workers negative constraints, not merely positive descriptions.
- Begin with a **manual one-slide simulation** before automating the whole agent system.

These are summaries, **not a complete, independently authenticated original Gemini transcript**.

## Human response and constraint evolution
The human sharpened the need-to-know boundary: **the supervisor does not need to know the creative prompt that produced the approved image**. Upstream creative generation and its complete prompt history stay available to authorized research reviewers; production sees only approved asset, copy, constraints and attestation. Creative options may be 1–4, with four the example trial default. Visual success is **meets/exceeds approved requirements**, not literal one-to-one NotebookLM replication.

## Core lesson
Constraints are revised when experience exposes a failure. The human does not merely relay a second model's approval; they *override, refine and authorize* the architecture. Discipline requires explicit inputs, scope limits and a falsifiable next proof.
