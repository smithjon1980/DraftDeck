## 2. Placement rules

1. Preserve a business-first narrative. The COO's first-store problem precedes vendor comparisons and AI adoption statistics.
2. Use the Boston University Questrom case-interview tradition only as inspiration for inquiry—clarifying assumptions, investigating alternatives, testing evidence, and supporting recommendations. Do not reproduce protected cases, diagrams, numbers, teaching materials, or third-party login details.
3. Give independent research an editorial **claim/source note**, not mandatory spoken exposition. Label sample, date, exact metric, observational limits and any conflicting definitions.
4. Distinguish the consultant's **in-story credibility** (demonstrating a disciplined method) from a claim that an external article is actually published, proprietary, validated, or successful.
5. Preserve human decision rights, versioned evidence and authorization boundaries in accordance with HAVE. No simulated action or model output counts as verified business execution.
6. Keep the future public lead-magnet article separate from the instructional manuscript. Public article/report production is unapproved.

## 5. Public source register and claim limits

- **Creatio, *The State of Enterprise AI Adoption* (2026):** vendor-produced survey of 608 IT/business leaders. Approximately 39% reported scaling or embedded AI, while 70% includes deployment in selected functions. Vendor commercial interest and observational limits must be disclosed. https://www.creatio.com/page/the-state-of-enterprise-ai-adoption/report
- **McKinsey, *The State of AI in 2026: On the Road to ROI*:** 44% of survey respondents report scaling AI across their enterprises; 37% attribute some positive EBIT impact. Survey definitions and respondents differ from Creatio's. https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai
- **Federal Reserve, *Monitoring AI Adoption in the U.S. Economy* (2026):** Census business-survey estimate of approximately 18% U.S. firms using AI as of end-2025. A separate Atlanta Fed executive survey underlies the 78% employment-weighted figure; never label that as a second Census measurement. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html
- **Boston University Questrom, *Case Interview Guide* (provided reference PDF):** framework inspiration only. Multiple third-party content and reuse constraints require rights review. Do not copy cases or exercises into public materials.

**Do not present 39%, 44% and 18% as directly comparable measures or average them.** Sample sizes, populations, geographies, dates, definitions and weighting differ. Do not use Creatio's 36% human-review response as a practice prevalence estimate: respondents selected a limited set of practices. The 33% versus 14% six-month revenue association rests on smaller subgroups (121 and 76); leave it out of the opening and avoid causal interpretations. No exact figures are needed in DU001–DU007.
