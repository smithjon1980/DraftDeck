# BOSS Guided Reasoning Format (GRF)

**Status:** Candidate Controlled Document (v1)
**Owner:** BOSS / Seven
**Authority chain:** DOCTRINE > POI > MODULE SOURCE > GENERATED MEDIA — GRF is an editorial standard; it governs form, never doctrine content.
**Governs:** magazines, student guides, workbook articles, newsletters, long-form explainers, and podcast/deck storylines produced under the BOSS Operator Program.

---

## 0. Purpose

> **Move the reader from a compelling question to an evidence-bounded conclusion through staged understanding, contrast, testing, application, and reflection.**

GRF is an epistemic editorial system. Its promise to the reader:

> **We will show you why an idea is attractive, what evidence it survives, what the governing rule actually establishes, what you can do with it, and where the conclusion stops.**

The GRF reasoning chain:

```text
QUESTION
↓
STRONGEST PLAUSIBLE CASE
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
PRESSURE TEST
↓
EVIDENCE STATE
↓
CANONICAL RULE
↓
OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE
↓
BOUNDARY
```

## 1. The Five Laws (Locked)

1. **Question before conclusion.**
2. **Represent the strongest reasonable case before testing it.**
3. **A claim earns space according to evidence, not rhetorical force.**
4. **Every explanation terminates in either an operator move, a boundary, or an explicitly unresolved state.**
5. **The reader never has to guess what is established, what is illustrative, and what remains unknown.**

---

## 2. Writing Voice Standard

Extracted mechanics, stated as BOSS rules:

- **Short intellectual steps.** One paragraph does one job.
- **Reader-facing questions.** Questions control progression; they are not decoration.
- **Plain words before technical words.** Define the concept in ordinary language, then introduce the formal term.
- **Strong first sentences.** Paragraphs begin with a compact claim, then explain it.
- **Progressive disclosure.** Never deliver the entire theory at once.
- **Concrete before abstract.** Scenarios, examples, and analogies carry explanatory weight.
- **Controlled repetition.** The core idea returns in headline, pull quote, body, caption, and summary.
- **Action after explanation.** Explanation resolves into practical steps, not description alone.

The voice rule:

> **Explain one move at a time. Ask before answering. Define before extending. Demonstrate before prescribing. Bound before concluding.**

## 3. GRF Paragraph Structure (Default)

```text
CLAIM
↓
WHY
↓
CONCRETE INSTANCE
↓
IMPLICATION
```

Specimen:

> A package must have a destination before it can be routed reliably. Without a defined destination, the system cannot determine which handling path is appropriate or what completion should mean. A request such as "improve this presentation" may therefore be too vague to route as one governed package. The operator must first define what successful delivery looks like.

Short, progressive, no throat-clearing.

## 4. Article Grammar — The Seven Beats

Every substantive article uses seven editorial beats:

```text
01 — THE QUESTION
02 — THE CASE
03 — THE PRESSURE TEST
04 — THE STANDARD
05 — THE WORKED EXAMPLE
06 — THE OPERATOR MOVE
07 — THE BOUNDARY
```

### 01 — THE QUESTION

Start with a question, tension, contradiction, or practical problem — never a topic announcement.

- Not: "Package decomposition is important."
- Instead: "When does a large task become too large to govern?"

### 02 — THE CASE

Present the strongest reasonable argument for the idea before criticizing it. Labels: WHY IT MAKES SENSE / THE CASE FOR IT. This prevents straw-manning and creates honest tension: the reader can see why an attractive answer might still be incomplete.

### 03 — THE PRESSURE TEST

Interrogate rather than conclude. Form:

```text
PRESSURE TEST
If this explanation were sufficient…
• What would we expect to observe?
• What evidence would contradict it?
• What happens at the boundary case?
```

Teaches the reader to adjudicate rather than receive assertions.

### 04 — THE STANDARD

Apply the governing authority: BOSS doctrine, evidence, engineering principle, source record, or established model. Labels: THE STANDARD / WHAT THE SYSTEM REQUIRES. The doctrinal sequence is:

```text
POPULAR / INTUITIVE CLAIM
↓
TEST
↓
CANONICAL STANDARD
```

Example:

```text
THE STANDARD
BOSS separates capability from permission.
CAN PROCESS ≠ MAY RECEIVE.
```

### 05 — THE WORKED EXAMPLE

Make it concrete. Devices: SCENARIO, ANALOGY, BEFORE/AFTER, WORKED CASE, MINI SIMULATION. Analogies remain subordinate to structural-correspondence rules — an analogy illustrates; it never establishes.

### 06 — THE OPERATOR MOVE

What the reader does differently now. Example:

```text
OPERATOR MOVE
Before routing:
1. identify the package;
2. establish its destination;
3. record unresolved operands;
4. attach handling requirements;
5. HOLD if required routing information is unknown.
```

Reading becomes operational capability.

### 07 — THE BOUNDARY

Labels: BOUNDARY CONDITIONS / DO NOT OVERREAD THIS. Explicitly prevents extending a principle beyond what has been established. Example:

```text
BOUNDARY
This establishes that capability and authorization are separate.
It does not establish:
• that every external handler is prohibited;
• that every local handler is authorized;
• that sensitivity alone determines route.
```

## 5. The Two Article Modes

### GRF-A — Adjudication Article

Used when examining a proposition:

```text
THE QUESTION
↓
THE CASE
↓
PRESSURE TEST
↓
THE STANDARD
↓
WORKED CASE
↓
BOTTOM LINE
↓
NEXT ROUTE
```

Example question: "Can a more capable model solve a bad package?"

### GRF-B — Operator Article

Used when teaching practical capability:

```text
THE PROBLEM
↓
DEFINE IT
↓
WHY IT HAPPENS
↓
DOES THE APPROACH HOLD?
↓
OPERATOR MOVE
↓
BOUNDARY
↓
PRACTICE
```

Example: "How to turn an ambiguous request into a bounded package."

A publication mixes modes deliberately; no article is forced into the wrong rhetorical shape.

## 6. GRF Issue Spine

Every issue carries one question or one operating problem — never a bundle of unrelated essays:

```text
COVER QUESTION / OPERATING PROBLEM
↓
ORIENTATION — why this matters
↓
MAP — what the reader will examine
↓
DEFINE — what the problem actually is
↓
EXAMINE — competing explanations / approaches
↓
TEST — what survives scrutiny
↓
APPLY — what the reader can actually do
↓
SYNTHESIZE — what the whole issue establishes
```

Reference 16–24 page issue structure:

```text
COVER        one strong question
P02          orientation + issue map
P03          DEFINE THE PROBLEM
P04–P07      ADJUDICATION ARTICLE 01
P08–P11      ADJUDICATION ARTICLE 02
P12–P15      OPERATOR ARTICLE
P16–P19      WORKED CASE / SPECIMEN
P20–P21      FIELD GUIDE / CHECKLIST
P22          SYNTHESIS
P23          NEXT ROUTE / RELATED MATERIAL
P24          BACK COVER QUESTION / SELF-CHECK
```

## 7. Page Grammar

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ VISUAL ANCHOR                          │
│ large image / diagram / specimen       │
├────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ EYEBROW                                │
│ MODULE / QUESTION / SERIES             │
│                                        │
│ BIG QUESTION OR CLAIM                  │
│                                        │
│ short orientation paragraph            │
├────────────────────┬───────────────────┤
│ PRIMARY NARRATIVE  │ SECONDARY LANE    │
│                    │                   │
│ body / reasoning   │ PRESSURE TEST     │
│                    │ DEFINITION        │
│                    │ FIELD NOTE        │
│                    │ BOUNDARY          │
├────────────────────┴───────────────────┤
│ OPERATOR MOVE / TAKEAWAY               │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The key principle:

> **The page has more than one reading speed.**

Skim lane: headline → question → pull quote → box → caption → takeaway.
Deep lane: the full primary narrative.

Page grammar composes under the BOSS composition standard (pure-white ground, near-black ink, restricted burnt-orange accent, drafting-gray substrate, stroke hierarchy). Editorial color-coding of sections uses accent discipline, not new palette.

## 8. Editorial Device Registry

House furniture — recognizable across every BOSS publication:

| Function | BOSS editorial label |
|---|---|
| Core opening problem | THE QUESTION |
| Charitable competing position | THE CASE |
| Reader interrogation | PRESSURE TEST |
| Definition | DEFINE IT |
| Doctrine / evidence | THE STANDARD |
| Concrete scenario | WORKED CASE |
| Quick evidence or statistic | SIGNAL |
| Counterexample | BREAK TEST |
| Action steps | OPERATOR MOVE |
| Caution | BOUNDARY |
| Short memorable conclusion | BOTTOM LINE |
| Forward link | NEXT ROUTE |

Registry rule: devices are added or renamed only by amendment to this document. Writers do not invent one-off device labels.

## 9. Compilation Targets

GRF compiles into:

- Student Operator Manual chapters (GRF-B dominant)
- Workbook articles (GRF-B)
- Magazine / newsletter issues (Issue Spine + mixed modes)
- Long-form explainers (GRF-A dominant)
- Podcast storylines (beats become conversational turns; canonical lines remain verbatim)
- Deck storylines (beats become slide arcs; page grammar becomes slide anatomy)
- Infographic spreads (SECONDARY LANE devices map to callout panels)

## 10. Boundary of This Standard

GRF governs editorial form and reading experience. It does not govern doctrine content, module objectives, or assessment. When a GRF artifact quotes a canonical statement, the wording is verbatim doctrine or it is marked as paraphrase. When a conclusion cannot be established, the artifact shows the unresolved state explicitly — Law 4 and Law 5.

> **TEST PASS ≠ DELIVERY ESTABLISHED** applies to reading too: an article the reader enjoyed is not an article the reader can operate from.
