# BOSS Development and Evidence Plan

Version: v1.0 candidate working plan.
Purpose: improve BOSS while developing the author's research practice in learning design and governed information work.
Scope: author development and project research. This is backstage material, separate from learner objectives, qualification rules and generation sources.

## Where the development work belongs

### 1. Beside each module source — design rationale
For each important teaching decision, record the learning problem, proposed mechanism, supporting source, alternative explanation, expected observation and boundary. Use the existing source objectives as the reference.

Initial Module 01 decision: use a shipper–clerk scene, explicit correspondence to information work and a subsequent unfamiliar information task. Proposed mechanism: learners identify relationships rather than memorize shipping vocabulary. This is a hypothesis; learner benefit is not established.

Project improvement: design choices become inspectable and easier to revise.
Author development: explain why a choice should work and what would challenge that explanation.
Artifact: a rationale entry linked to the module revision.

### 2. Beside the editorial standard — literature and claim review
Maintain a literature record that distinguishes an empirical finding, a theoretical account, practice guidance and an author hypothesis. For each source record question, population/context, method, finding, limits and relevance to the particular BOSS decision. Seek competing explanations and negative or mixed findings as well as supporting work.

The initial course comparison is useful positioning. It is not a systematic literature review or evidence of novelty. Document the search terms and selection rationale when building the research review; describe its actual coverage.

Project improvement: explanations gain a traceable basis and appropriate limits.
Author development: read methods and evidence critically instead of using citations as endorsements.
Artifact: a source-to-claim record with unresolved questions.

### 3. Beside the podcast and slide briefs — representation audit
Trace the same objective, key relationships, canonical statements, example facts and boundaries across the written section, podcast and slide companion. Record which version of each medium actually exists and was used. The podcast explains story and reasoning; slides expose architecture; the workbook tests application. Medium-specific presentation may differ while the underlying claims remain aligned.

Check agent = service-counter/admission/routing responsibility; model = processing capability; data/task = payload. The learner remains a person governing information work. A shipping payment does not establish AI permission, and receipt alone does not establish factual correctness.

Project improvement: prevents cross-media drift and literalization of the metaphor.
Author development: distinguish a useful representation from evidence that it teaches effectively.
Artifact: a correspondence record with actual artifact references and open mismatches. Do not invent slide numbers or timestamps before compilation.

### 4. Beside the assessment package — prospective study protocol
Use the existing Module 01 pilot and transfer plan as the first feasibility study. Before collecting data, record the question, task/rubric versions, instructional exposure, sampling rationale, assistance conditions, access provisions, intended measures and analysis approach. Version later changes; preserve their reasons.

RQ1: After this Module 01 teaching sequence, how do novice AI users distinguish required unresolved conditions from fully established permitted actions on unfamiliar information tasks?

RQ2: What misconceptions and assessor disagreements appear, and what revisions do they suggest?

Forms A/B require scoped HOLD and refusal of an ineligible transfer; Form C supplies sufficient conditions and permits a public-input handler. Use the published form-specific scoring adaptations. This checks conditional judgment rather than rewarding an automatic HOLD response.

The existing proposed 6–10 learner cohort is a feasibility starting point, not a power calculation. Record actual experience rather than assuming all participants are novices. Use consenting participants, pseudonymous records and declared data access/retention arrangements. For later institution-linked research, establish the applicable review requirements before recruitment.

Project improvement: questions and measures are decided before seeing favorable results.
Author development: operationalize concepts, justify methods and anticipate alternative explanations.
Artifact: a frozen protocol version and an administration record.

### 5. Beside the pilot records — analysis and revision log
Keep first independent assessor ratings distinct from reconciled scores. Report criterion counts with denominators, critical errors, inappropriate action dispositions, missing/invalid administrations, assistance and instructional exposure. Report delayed transfer separately. Existing task rules remain provisional; this research layer creates no new passing threshold.

For each proposed revision record the observed problem, evidence, competing explanation, change, expected effect and follow-up task. Preserve the earlier version and its results. Improvement after feedback does not rewrite the first independent attempt.

The current before/after feasibility design can reveal usability issues and performance patterns. It does not isolate the shipping analogy's causal effect, establish equivalent forms, or prove superiority to another course. A later comparison study requires its own design and justification.

Project improvement: changes respond to documented problems.
Author development: analyze uncertainty and explain conclusions proportionately.
Artifact: a pilot report, revision decision and next-cycle question.

### 6. Beside the release review — contribution and independent critique
Write a bounded account of the problem, prior work, method, results, limitations and candidate contribution. Original terminology or a distinctive artifact is not sufficient evidence of an original research contribution. Explain what the findings add to existing understanding and which contexts they cover.

Seek critique from people with relevant learning-design, measurement or research-methods expertise. Record the objection, evidence examined, response and resulting revision. AI-assisted preparation is useful; document that assistance and demonstrate that the author can explain and defend the reasoning independently.

Project improvement: stronger review and a clearer account of what BOSS establishes.
Author development: scholarly argument, revision under criticism and accountable authorship.
Artifact: a contribution memo and response-to-review record.

## Development progression
These are proposed project milestones, not academic grades or degree equivalence.

DRAFTED: a traceable artifact and rationale exist; the author can explain the decision and identify unresolved questions.
TESTED: the relevant claim or method has been challenged with appropriate observations or worked evidence; limits and revisions are recorded.
INDEPENDENTLY REVIEWED: a relevant reviewer has examined the work, and the author has addressed material objections. Review establishes only the stated scope.

Track each area separately. Evidence for a revised claim may require reopening an earlier milestone. Preserve progress history rather than assigning a percentage of PhD completion.

## First author mission — Module 01 research brief
Complete one brief using the compiled Module 01 section and existing pilot instruments:

- Define the observable decisions that would count as adequate conditional judgment, using the existing rubric and form adaptations.
- Identify a relevant prior finding or theory, explain its limits, and state what remains a BOSS hypothesis.
- Identify observations that would challenge the teaching design, including inappropriate HOLD on the fully specified public case.
- Explain what the feasibility pilot could establish and what requires a different study.

Completion evidence: an understandable brief, source-to-claim trace, justified measurement plan and recorded critique. Drafting this brief alone establishes no learner outcomes.

## Research basis and limits
The Design-Based Research Collective (2003), Design-Based Research: An Emerging Paradigm for Educational Inquiry, Educational Researcher 32(1), 5–8. The paper connects theoretical design claims with cycles of implementation, analysis and revision, and treats context and processes as important to interpreting outcomes. It also distinguishes this research ambition from simply improving an artifact. This supports the proposed workflow; it does not establish BOSS's effectiveness.
https://designbasedresearch.org/reppubs/DBRC2003.pdf

This is one starter source, not a completed literature synthesis. Analogy/transfer, multimedia learning and educational measurement require further primary-source review before their findings are used to justify specific BOSS claims. External scholarly frameworks inform instruction and research; they do not silently amend BOSS doctrine.
