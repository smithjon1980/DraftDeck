### Scene 01 — A Business That Is Busy but Not Moving Forward

**001.** At Main Street Copy, orders are coming in, staff members are busy, and the owner still spends the final hour of each day resolving problems that were supposed to be finished. A customer returns because the wrong version of a document was printed; another asks why a packed order has not reached its destination. The owner's first instinct is to buy better equipment and hire another employee. The visiting consultant begins somewhere else: with **SEVEN—Survey, Evaluate, Validate, Execute, Normalize**—the established discipline for investigating an operating problem before authorizing a solution. During Survey, the consultant asks where each kind of problem actually belongs, introducing **PARCELS: Platform, Attachment, Routing, Carriage, Exchange, Language, and Service**. These are seven architectural responsibilities, not seven physical counters in the store. They distinguish whether a failure concerns the available systems, the identity or integrity of a job, its destination, its movement, a handoff, the meaning of instructions, or the service ultimately accepted by the customer. Before proposing automation, the consultant must locate the defect, separate observed facts from assumptions, and determine which evidence is missing.

**002.** The owner points to a shipping label and says, “That order was completed yesterday.” But a printed label does not establish carrier custody, and a completed production task does not establish customer acceptance. The consultant introduces **PRIME—Package, Route, Inspect, Move, Establish Delivery**—as the operational sequence for an accountable job. First, define the authorized order and the material that belongs to it; next, select an eligible destination and handling path; inspect identity, instructions, condition, and permissions; carry out the permitted movement; then establish delivery using evidence appropriate to the receiving boundary. **PARCELS answers where a concern belongs, PRIME governs how the work moves, and SEVEN governs how the consultant investigates and improves the operation.** The next step is a bounded diagnostic, not a sales pitch: trace one permitted order from intake to acceptance, identify the point where its record and reality diverge, test the competing explanations, and submit the findings to the owner for a decision. The learner will practice that same reasoning before recommending a capability or asking for implementation authority.

### Scripted dialogue — separate teaching apparatus (not a numbered paragraph)

**Owner:** “I thought a shipping label meant the order was done.”

**Consultant:** “It means a label was created. What record shows the carrier accepted the parcel?”

**Owner:** “We haven't checked that.”

**Consultant:** “Then we have an evidence gap, not proof of delivery. Let's follow the order through the handoffs before we spend money on a new system.”
