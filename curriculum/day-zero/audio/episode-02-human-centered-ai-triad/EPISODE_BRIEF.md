# Episode 02 — The Human-Centered AI Triad

**Place in series:** immediately after *The Discipline of Constraint: From a Single Note to a Governed Symphony* (DZ-AUDIO-01). Not a competing production priority today.

## Core educational question

**Does a human directing two AI models create a more disciplined and defensible way to tackle sustained project work than unreviewed interaction with one model?** This is a **testable instructional/research hypothesis**, not an established improvement claim.

## Original human framing

Human's role notation: **A = ChatGPT, B = the human, C = Gemini.** Human in the middle keeps the parties in sync (an informal *NSYNC* boy-band analogy), not as passive message carrier but as a person who sets purpose and constraints, challenges both models, accepts or rejects their suggestions, and retains final authority. The humorous "AI threesome" is a *creative title candidate*, not the formal research term or a requirement that every ordinary request use two models.

A: formalizes, proposes plans, and can carry out authorized tool operations when connected.
B: human/operator defines real-world intent, compares claims, provides source context, decides boundaries, corrects, validates, authorizes.
C: Gemini provides alternate critique, architectural diagnostics and research judgment when supplied the actual authorized context.
Roles are **descriptive and interchangeable by assignment**, not proof of model independence or reliable factual correctness.

## Governing relationships

Episode 01 teaches **constraint → discipline → sustained practice → evaluated results**, with verification throughout. Episode 02 examines how the human exercises that discipline when two models propose different plans—or agree on the same mistake. Human authority is not equivalent to evidence; independent source checks and real executions remain necessary.

The same case history supports both episodes, but not the same assessment:
- Episode 01: explain *why* source and operating constraints matter and what verified feedback changes.
- Episode 02: demonstrate *who* makes a decision, how proposals are reconciled and what evidence is required before action.

## Suggested learner exercise — draft only

Present one bounded project request and two conflicting, clearly labeled *candidate* model recommendations derived from real, permitted evidence. Ask the learner to:
1. Identify what each model claims and what evidence each lacks.
2. Set the problem boundary and scope of the next test.
3. Decide which suggestion, if any, should advance; record why and reject unsupported claims.
4. Request or conduct an observable test; classify as PASS, HOLD, FAIL or NOT_ESTABLISHED.
5. Archive a compact prompt → response → human decision → evidence record.

**Do not** present staged fictional agent outputs as historical Gemini transcripts. If synthetic role-play is pedagogically useful, label it SYNTHETIC.
