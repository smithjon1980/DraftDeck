## Composed Manuscript Demonstration — 001–003

### 001 — Monday, 6:47 A.M.
The parking garage beneath the company's Dallas headquarters was nearly empty when the executive pulled into his reserved space. He remained behind the wheel, studying an email that had arrived shortly before midnight. The subject line read *Texas Campus — Executive Decision Required*. Attached was a rendering of a proposed corporate campus designed to accommodate four thousand employees initially, with room for ten thousand over time. Glass buildings, landscaped courtyards, shaded pedestrian corridors, and a grand entrance filled the screen. The proposal looked remarkably complete. Yet the executive could not remember anyone establishing precisely which business problem the development was supposed to solve. He turned off the engine, collected his briefcase, and headed toward the elevator.

### 002 — The Question Before the Meeting
His executive assistant had prepared the morning briefing. It contained projected employment growth, recruitment advantages, regional economic benefits, and potential savings from consolidating offices. Each argument sounded reasonable, but the executive noticed that they were being presented as though they described the same objective. He asked which outcome the board had actually authorized the team to pursue. His assistant searched the meeting records. The board had approved further investigation, but the available minutes did not identify a single primary business outcome. The architects would arrive in forty minutes. The executive wondered whether the meeting was intended to review a building or discover why the company wanted one.

### 003 — A Building Without an Agreement
At nine o'clock, the architectural rendering filled the conference room wall. Facilities described consolidation. Human resources emphasized recruitment. Technology wanted modern infrastructure. Finance questioned the capital commitment. The executive listened until the architect reached a slide labeled *Strategic Alignment*. He asked which single objective would justify the investment if the company could accomplish only one. The room fell quiet. When the directors finally answered, their priorities differed. The executive left the rendering on the screen and asked each department to explain its intended outcome. The meeting had not produced a building decision. It had exposed a question the company needed to answer first.

## AI Principle 01 — Define the Problem Before the Prompt
A large language model generates responses using instructions and context. A detailed request can produce a polished answer even when the underlying business objective is unclear. In this scene, the architectural rendering is not evidence of aligned objectives. Likewise, a convincing AI-generated plan does not establish that the operator supplied the correct problem, complete context, or authorized requirements.

**Operator responsibility:** Define what successful work would accomplish.

## BOSS Framework — Operator → Context → Task → Verification
This is a **teaching model for this exercise**, not a verified canonical repository component.

- **Operator:** Establishes the objective.
- **Context:** Supplies relevant records.
- **Task:** Defines the requested output.
- **Verification:** Checks the result against evidence.

## Pause & Consider — Study Cycle 01
1. What had the board actually authorized? **Answer:** Further investigation of the proposal.
2. Why was the rendering insufficient evidence of strategic alignment? **Answer:** It did not establish agreement on the business objective.
3. What should the AI operator establish before requesting a campus investment recommendation? **Answer:** The desired business outcome and decision criteria.

## Inspect the Payload — Practical AI Exercise

**Request A — Underspecified**

> Create a business plan for our new corporate campus.

**Request B — Evidence-grounded**

> Review the available board minutes and departmental objectives. Identify which business outcomes are explicitly authorized, which are proposed, and which remain unresolved. Do not assume that approval to investigate constitutes approval to build. Produce a comparison table, cite the supporting records, and list the questions requiring executive resolution.

**Evidence Window:** In this fictional case, the minutes support investigation, not construction. An AI assistant could help compare documents but could not establish additional authorization without supporting evidence.

**Carry Forward:** A well-structured prompt cannot repair missing source information. The next study cycle examines incomplete, inconsistent, or unauthorized evidence.
